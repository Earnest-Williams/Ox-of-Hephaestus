# Proposal: the player is the Master of the Ox

**Status:** proposed, 2 November 2026 — for discussion, not accepted.
**Scope:** the player's role and the interface that follows from it. It
deliberately ignores what the current code can or cannot do yet.

**Mock-up:** six linked, clickable screens on a Claude design canvas:
<https://claude.ai/artifact/J6JVNjWGhRdDKKRARp6ref> (opens only once the
owner shares it). The canvas sources are kept in [mockup/](mockup/) for
reference; they need the canvas runtime to render.

## The role

The player is the **Master** (captain) of the Ox. **You command people, not
machines.** You never open a valve yourself. Every lever you pull becomes an
instruction to a crew member, who interprets it, may push back, carries it
out in their own way, and remembers how you treated them.

This leans on what makes Ox different — separate crew minds that act, learn
and remember — instead of working around it. The optional sixth captain actor
is the seat the player takes.

## Three rules for every screen

1. **You only know what a captain could know.** Every reading names its
   source and age: a camera, a door log, a crew report, an instrument.
   Instruments drift and fail; crew reports can be wrong or late.
2. **No mood or stat bars for people.** You see behaviour ("short with Teo at
   handover", "hasn't eaten in nine hours") and the history between you.
   Skills appear only as work the world has assessed and logged.
3. **Time is the main resource.** Pause, or run at 1×, 10×, 60× or 600×.
   The clock stops itself on the conditions you choose.

## The screens

| Screen | What the player does there |
|---|---|
| **Bridge** | Time controls; crew as last seen; a bow-to-drive section of the ship; a queue of decisions waiting on you; the ship's log. |
| **Orders** | Who, what, where and why; priority; a **latitude** slider ("exactly as written" → "whatever it takes"); a **personal risk** slider; report-back triggers. A preview shows how that person will probably read it and reply. |
| **Ship** | Reactor output and power circuits, cabin oxygen and temperature, rations, with live knock-on effects (food days, battery state, whether Hold 3 can stay cold). Changes are drafted and sent to the chief engineer, who applies them. |
| **Crew** | One person as you know them: observations with sources, a logbook of proven skills, the history between you, and conversation (suggested lines or your own words). |
| **Rota** | A 24-hour watch plan showing where people drift from it and where coverage has gaps; standing orders as switches; auto-pause triggers. |
| **Voyage** | The route and its events, a pace slider (save reaction mass ↔ arrive sooner), an optional supply detour, and the contract with its deadlines and penalties. |

### The player's verbs

- **Decide** — approve, deny, delay or question what the crew bring to you.
- **Order** — a task, with how much freedom and risk you allow.
- **Set policy** — power, air, warmth, rations, pace: carried out by crew.
- **Make rules** — standing orders that act while you are asleep or busy.
- **Talk** — ask, thank, correct, rest, teach; build or spend trust.
- **Schedule** — the watch rota, and who rests when.
- **Manage time** — how fast the voyage runs and what stops it.

## The sample situation in the mock-up

All six screens share one moment, Day 41, so the dilemmas are concrete:
Ari has gone quiet in Hold 3 just as its cultures warm towards spoiling and
its only working sensor is overdue for calibration; Mara is 15 hours awake
with a failing coolant pump; Iona needs a correction burn approved; and at
full rations the food runs out 24 days before arrival unless you ration or
detour for supplies.

Crew names follow the existing roles. Their jobs, every number and the
contract terms are placeholders.

## Open questions

1. **Captain, or something else?** Alternatives: the ship's own mind
   (sees everything, can touch nothing); a distant owner with signal delay;
   one crew member among equals.
2. **How much does conversation carry?** Central here; it could be
   occasional, with orders and policy doing most of the work.
3. **What counts as winning** beyond delivering the contract — for example, a
   crew that wants to sail with you again?
4. **How much should the order preview reveal** about how someone will react,
   given rule 2?
