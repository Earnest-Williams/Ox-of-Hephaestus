## Protocol

Six actual distinct actor brains after the authenticated G9 voyage, with private chronological memories and original native Concordia observations, were evaluated in six explicitly *hypothetical* decision situations (restraint record accuracy, another scrubber alarm, testimony conflicts, watch handover, last life-support spare, fatigued watch). The experiment counterbalanced **three displayed option orders**, and **indexed-memory projection on/off**, for **216 total real Decider Q4_K_M calls** and **108 within-actor memory comparisons**. Separately, **9,216 deterministic personality-routing draws** tested configuration of GOAP-vs-Decider consultation rates. No model comparison was performed. Every G10 action was a no-world planning proposal.

These G10 alternatives were direct native Decider tests, not real world work attempts.

### Limitations and engineering conclusions

The **32/72** order-sensitive triples represent a real reliability limitation: prompts and action-list order still influence decisions, especially under fatigue and scarcity. A character's memorable deviation should not be confused with a stable personality trait until it persists under phrasing and order controls. All explicitly prohibited actions being rejected is reassuring for the offered scenarios but does not establish broader safety or realism. Both Decider and GOAP were choosing from finite authored possibilities.

The Ox should retain truthful provenance (server-issued world receipts versus merely witnessed speech), and each actor's private revisions. The immediately following G11 experiment tests consequences of **actual world-authorized social agreement choices**, in contrast to G10's isolated counterfactual proposals.
